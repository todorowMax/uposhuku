# Architecture

- ARCH-1. S: one component, hook or module has one responsibility (render a section, own a form, load data); when a file does two, split by responsibility, not by line count. Why: `ProfileCatalog.tsx` rendered, fetched and held state for a whole page. Check: review; ESLint `max-lines` (COMP-3) and `sonarjs/cognitive-complexity` (ARCH-7) are only smoke detectors.
- ARCH-2. O: extend through variants (`cva`), props, slots and composition; never edit a primitive for one feature, and when a component gets a third boolean prop for a new case, replace the booleans with a variant. Why: per-case props and edits fork the primitive. Check: review. Example: ukoshiku/components/ui/button.tsx
- ARCH-3. L: a wrapper around a primitive accepts the primitive's props (`React.ComponentProps<typeof X>`), spreads the rest, and forwards `ref`, `className` (through `cn`) and `aria-*`, so it can replace the primitive anywhere. Why: a wrapper that swallows props breaks callers silently. Check: review, `tsc`. Example: Demo/DemoWorkSpace/components/ui/close-button.tsx
- ARCH-4. I: keep prop interfaces small and pass what is used: two fields of a record means two props, not the record, and a component past about seven props is split or grouped. Why: god-object props couple a component to data it never reads. Check: review.
- ARCH-5. D: UI depends on hooks, not on concrete clients; data access lives in `lib/`, hooks call it, and presentational components never call `fetch`. Why: a deep component that fetches cannot be reused or tested without the network. Check: ESLint `no-restricted-syntax` on `fetch(` in `components/**` outside `use-*.ts`.
- ARCH-6. DRY: extract on the third repetition, not the second; two similar pieces may stay copies until a third shows what really varies. Why: a premature abstraction costs more than a short duplicate. Check: review.
- ARCH-7. KISS: take the simplest design that passes the case; keep cognitive complexity at the rule's default 15 or less and nesting at 3 or less. Inline handlers inside a component inflate its cognitive complexity: move them into the component's hook (COMP-2) instead of raising the limit. Why: branching depth is where state bugs hide. Check: ESLint `sonarjs/cognitive-complexity` 15 (eslint-plugin-sonarjs 4.x) and `max-depth` 3, as warnings that prompt a responsibility check.
- ARCH-8. YAGNI: no speculative options, props, parameters, config keys or extension points for cases nobody asked for. Why: unused flexibility has to be read, tested and kept. Check: `knip`, ESLint `@typescript-eslint/no-unused-vars`.
- ARCH-9. Composition over inheritance: compose with `children`, slots and hooks; no `class` components or inheritance chains. Excepted: `Error` subclasses (any class whose base name ends in `Error`) and the Cloudflare bases the platform requires (`WorkflowEntrypoint`, `DurableObject`, `WorkerEntrypoint`, `RpcTarget`). Why: inheritance ties behaviour to a hierarchy that composition keeps flat. Check: ESLint `no-restricted-syntax` on `ClassDeclaration[superClass]` other than the excepted bases.
- ARCH-10. Logic is pure functions: calculations, mapping and formatting take inputs and return values, with no hidden reads of `Date.now()`, `Math.random()`, `window` or storage; effects stay at the edges (hooks, handlers). Why: pure functions are testable without mocks. Check: review, unit tests.
- ARCH-11. Use early returns and guard clauses instead of nested `if/else`. Why: the main path stays at one indent level. Check: ESLint `max-depth` 3, `no-else-return`.
- ARCH-12. A function takes at most three parameters; beyond that it takes one options object. Why: positional arguments past three get swapped silently. Check: ESLint `max-params` 3, as a warning.
